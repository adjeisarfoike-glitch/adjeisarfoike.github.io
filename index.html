Thesis and essays

There’s a question I keep coming back to: when the answer comes from a machine, how do we decide whether to believe it? My thesis takes that question to college students. My essays are where I think out loud about AI, online platforms and the companies behind them.

My master’s thesis

How undergraduates judge the credibility of generative AI

Look over a student’s shoulder these days and there’s a good chance ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini is open in another tab. Every answer those tools give asks for a quick judgment: do I trust this? I want to understand how students actually make that call.

I’m working from S. Shyam Sundar’s MAIN model, which suggests that the features of a technology can set off mental shortcuts. I’m focusing on two. One is about form: whether the way a tool presents information makes it feel more real. The other is about agency: whether knowing a machine, not a person, is doing the answering makes it feel more objective.

Instead of measuring those shortcuts from a distance, I’m sitting down with undergraduates and asking them to talk me through the AI tool they use most — what makes them believe it, and when they stop.

Texas State University, 2026–27 · Advised by Dr. Heidi Makady

Profile IIIsaac Sarfo Adjei

I make persuasive messages, and I study them

I am a second-year master’s student in digital media communication at Texas State University in San Marcos. Before that, I studied Publishing Studies at Kwame Nkrumah University of Science and Technology (KNUST) in Ghana.

Most days I’m at the School of Journalism and Mass Communication, where I’m a graduate assistant. I help with a course called AI and Automation for Media Professionals and look after the school’s web content, so I see the questions my thesis asks play out every week.

I also design. The posters and the Save Asaase Yaa illustrations further down are that side of me, and making persuasive messages has taught me a lot about how to take them apart. Now I’m applying to PhD programs in communication and advertising, hoping to keep doing both and to build a life in teaching and research.

Making persuasive messages taught me how to take them apart.
