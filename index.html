Case study ISave Asaase Yaa

Save Asaase Yaa

Galamsey — illegal small-scale mining — has reached 34 of Ghana's 288 forest reserves, devastating 4,726 hectares, and has left rivers like the Pra, Ankobra and Birim too turbid to treat. The numbers are public. The problem is that nobody feels them. This campaign was built to close that gap.

The brief

Ghanaians already know galamsey is happening. Coverage is constant and largely statistical, which makes it easy to absorb and easy to ignore. The campaign needed to move the issue from something read about to something implicated in.

The constraint

No budget, no client, no distribution beyond social. Anything made had to survive being seen at thumbnail size on a phone, in a feed, next to everything else competing for the same three seconds.

The decision

Four images rather than a campaign of many, sequenced as an argument: the forest before clearing, the river during, the child after, and finally the buyer — the only person in the sequence with a choice. The last image reframes the viewer from witness to participant.
